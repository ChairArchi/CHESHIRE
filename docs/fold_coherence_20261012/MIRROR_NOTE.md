**Source facts.** A coarse polygon mesh is repeatedly cut by a plane, reflected and joined by Boolean union. The largest connected part is retained, then used as the control mesh for final Catmull-Clark refinement. Fig. 1's **21 iterations are cut/mirror iterations**, not 21 CC refinements. Plane order, direction and position change the outcome (Fig. 3). The method assumes valid closed input and nondegenerate operations; convex polygons are required before subdivision. Arbitrary curved reflection can sacrifice manifoldness. The authors explicitly acknowledge limited local control without global changes. This is neither Mandelbulb growth nor a physical folding solver.

**CHESHIRE inference.** The useful distinction is that connected motifs and cavities arise in the coarse control structure before their translation into a continuous subdivision surface. This supports examining our control grammar and inherited local relationships instead of covering noisy cell offsets with smoothing or simply increasing fractal strength. It does not supply a drop-in repair for the current near-view facets, recovered IAAC weights or guaranteed crease memory. A new mirror/Boolean engine is unnecessary for the ongoing CURVED comparison; retain this as a separate future grammar reference.
